---
title: "Threat detection"
description: "Spot malware and data theft in your DNS traffic: generated domains, DNS tunnels, known-bad domains from threat feeds, unusual clients and newly seen domains, with alerts and optional blocking."
---

Malware has to use DNS. It looks up its command server, often among hundreds of random-looking
names; it hides stolen data in the names it asks for; it calls hosts that researchers already know
are bad. DNZ watches the queries it answers for these signs. What it finds is a **finding** for a
client and a domain, with the evidence behind it, and you decide what to do: look into it, mute it,
allow the domain, or block it.

Threat detection lives under **DNS → Threats** (superadmins), and in the CLI under `dnz threats`.

<Note>
  Detection never slows answers down. The detectors get a copy of the query log as it is written,
  on their own task; when they fall behind, queries are skipped and counted instead of waiting.
  Queries for your own zones are never suspicious. Their memory stays level however many clients
  there are: for 2,000 clients at 1,000 queries a second, about 100 MB.
</Note>

## The detectors

Each detector has a **score**, a **threshold** (from which the score counts) and a **time window**.
Windows are fixed: a 10-minute window runs from :00 to :10, :10 to :20, and so on.

| Detector | Looks for | Score | Threshold | Window | Alerts | May block |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Threat feeds** (`feed`) | Hosts on a threat feed, and their subdomains | Queries to listed hosts from one client | 1 | 10 min | yes | yes |
| **DNS tunneling** (`tunnel`) | Data carried in names | The strongest sign over its limit | 1.0 | 5 min | yes | yes |
| **Generated domains** (`dga`) | Machine-made names | 0 (a name people pick) to 1 (random letters) | 0.65 | 10 min | yes | yes |
| **Unusual clients** (`baseline`) | Clients far busier than usual | Times the client's usual upper level | 5 | 10 min | yes | no |
| **Newly seen domains** (`new_domain`) | Domains no client asked for before | Queries in the window after the first | 1 | 60 min | no | no |

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Threat feeds">
    A query for a host on one of your feeds, or a subdomain of it, is a finding for that host. Feeds
    are lists by URL (hosts files, AdGuard lists or one domain per line). The cluster leader
    downloads them every 2 hours, and every node loads them from the database.

    - **abuse.ch URLhaus** (hosts serving malware now) and **ThreatFox** (command servers and
      botnets) want a free key per user: their old public download links answer `410 Gone`. Sign
      in at [auth.abuse.ch](https://auth.abuse.ch/), then paste the hostfile download URL from the
      URLhaus or ThreatFox download page (it carries your key), or the URL and your **Auth-Key**.
    - **HaGeZi Threat Intelligence (mini)** needs no key and combines many feeds (about 200,000
      hosts).
    - Any other list you trust, by URL.

    Feeds only *detect*. To block every listed host outright, add the same list under
    [Filtering](/dns/filtering) as a blocklist.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="DNS tunneling">
    Tools like iodine and dnscat2 carry data in the names they ask for
    (`aGVsbG8gd29y.t.example.com`) and get it back in the answers, so every query is a new name
    under one domain. Per client and domain, the detector counts in the window, for **different
    names only**:

    | Sign | Limit |
    | --- | --- |
    | Different names | 150 |
    | Encoded-looking names (a label of 24+ characters with 3.4+ bits of entropy per character, or 60+ characters of subdomain) | 25 |
    | Different TXT, NULL or CNAME names | 60 |
    | Answer bytes of different names | 256 KiB |

    The score is the highest count over its limit: 2.4 means one sign is 2.4 times its limit. The
    same long name asked over and over (a load balancer, an API host) counts once.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Generated domains (DGA)">
    Malware with a domain generation algorithm looks up many random-looking names until one answers.
    DNZ scores the label of the registered domain (`kqsbtbelblncoy` of `www.kqsbtbelblncoy.com`):
    60% how unlikely its letters are under a trigram model trained on the 10,000 most popular
    domains, 25% its longest run of consonants, 5% its entropy and 10% digits mixed into letters.

    A client is flagged when it asks for **3 or more** names scoring 0.65 or more in the window, so
    one odd name never alerts. Short labels, IDNs and popular domains are not scored. On 20,000 real
    domains outside the training set, 0.7% score 0.65 or more; of random 10–20 letter labels, about
    80% do.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Unusual clients">
    For every client DNZ learns its usual number of queries per window, and how many domains new to
    it it usually asks for. "New to it" means not asked for lately: DNZ remembers the last one to two
    million client and domain pairs, in a fixed amount of memory however many clients there are. After 36 active windows, a window counts when the client sent at least
    1,000 queries and that is 5 times its usual upper level (mean + 3 standard deviations), or asked
    for at least 200 new domains, 5 times its usual. Windows without queries don't count, so a laptop
    that sleeps at night isn't judged against its nights.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Newly seen domains">
    A registered domain (by the Public Suffix List, bundled) that no client of this server, or of
    any node of the cluster, asked for before. For the first 72 hours domains are only learnt. New
    domains are common, so this detector doesn't alert or block by default; raise its threshold (say
    20 queries within the hour) to catch only domains asked for over and over.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Get started

  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Add a threat feed">
        Open **DNS → Threats**. In **Threat feeds**, click **HaGeZi Threat Intelligence**, or
        **abuse.ch URLhaus** / **ThreatFox** and paste the download URL from your abuse.ch account.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Get alerts">
        Under **Settings → Notifications**, tick **Threat found** on a provider. New providers have
        it on already.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Check that it works">
        **Check that it works** on the Threats page has commands to paste on a client, with your
        server's address filled in. Within a few seconds a finding shows up.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    ```bash
    dnz threats feeds add "HaGeZi TIF mini" https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hagezi/dns-blocklists/main/adblock/tif.mini.txt
    dnz notify set 1 --event down --event recovered --event threat
    dnz threats list
    ```
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

To see a finding right away, run this on a machine that uses DNZ (with your server's address):

```bash
for i in $(seq 1 60); do
  dig @192.0.2.53 +short TXT "$(head -c 30 /dev/urandom | od -An -tx1 | tr -d ' \n').tunnel-test.example"
done
```

## Findings

A finding is one detector, one client and one domain (none for unusual clients). It keeps
counting while it happens: hits, the highest and the last score, first and last seen, the latest
sample queries (name, type, response code, time) and the detector's numbers. Every node of a
[cluster](/deploy/cluster) adds to the same findings. The page groups them **per client** (which
machines to look at) or **per domain** (which domains several machines ask for), newest activity
first and a page at a time (25, 50 or 100 rows; the page number stays in the address). The search,
status and detector filters apply before the page is cut. An opened group shows its newest 100
findings, with **Show more** for the rest.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **new** | Not looked at yet; alerts when it is created |
| **acknowledged** | Someone is on it |
| **muted** | Keeps counting, never alerts or blocks |
| **resolved** | Dealt with; becomes **new** again (and alerts) if it happens again |

- **Mute rules** match a detector, a client (address or network) and a domain, for a while or for
  good. New findings that match are created muted.
- The **allow list** holds domains no detector should ever flag. Adding one resolves its open
  findings and lifts its block. Reverse lookups, local names, mail blocklists (Spamhaus and
  others) and antivirus lookups are left alone out of the box.
- Findings not seen for 30 days are deleted.

## Alerts

New and reopened findings go to the notification providers that subscribe to **Threat found**
(`threat`), with the evidence and sample queries; webhooks get everything as JSON. So one noisy
client doesn't flood them, each server sends at most **one alert per client every 30 minutes** and
**20 an hour** in all; the client's next alert says how many were held back.

## Automatic blocking

Off by default. Turned on, a new finding of the feed, tunnel or DGA detector blocks its domain for
24 hours, through a list DNZ manages that [filtering](/dns/filtering) loads server-wide. Popular and
allowed domains are never blocked automatically, and your own allow rules still win. You can also
block a finding's domain by hand with **Block domain**.

## Tuning

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Tunnel findings for a backup agent or telemetry | Allow its domain |
| A load test or crawler looks like a tunnel | Mute the client, or raise the different-names limit |
| DGA findings for real sites | Allow the domain; with many, raise the threshold to 0.75 or the domain count to 5 |
| Unusual-client findings at the start of the day | Raise the minimum queries, or the threshold to 8 |
| Nothing from the feed detector | Check the feeds for errors (an abuse.ch feed without its key) |

The settings are on the Threats page, with `dnz threats settings`, or through the
[API](/api/threats). Every node reads them again within 15 seconds.

## Metrics

`/metrics` has `dnz_threat_*`: queries looked at and dropped, hits and new findings per detector,
alerts sent and held back, findings by detector and status, feed age and size, and blocked domains.
The bundled Prometheus rules alert on new feed, tunnel and DGA findings, a feed older than a day,
and detection falling behind. See [Prometheus](/dashboard/prometheus).
