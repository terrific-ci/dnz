---
title: "DNSSEC"
description: "Sign a local zone with DNSSEC so validating resolvers can verify that DNZ's answers are genuine."
---

DNSSEC adds signatures to DNS answers. Validating resolvers (Google `8.8.8.8`, Cloudflare
`1.1.1.1`, Quad9, modern ISPs) check them against a **DS record** at your domain registrar, so
nobody in between can forge an answer for your zone. DNZ signs the answers of local zones, one zone
at a time.

To have DNZ check the signatures of other domains it resolves for your clients, see
[DNSSEC validation](/dns/dnssec-validation).

<Note>
  DNSSEC is only useful when DNZ is the zone's **public nameserver**. For zones on Cloudflare, turn
  on DNSSEC at Cloudflare. Internal zones (such as `corp.local`) have no public DS; internal
  resolvers can use their DNSKEY as a trust anchor if needed.
</Note>

## Turn it on

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on DNSSEC for the zone">
    Open the zone under **DNS → Zones** and turn on **DNSSEC** (needs the *Manage zones*
    permission). To sign with something other than the defaults, open **Signing settings** first
    (see [below](#signing-settings)). Or from the terminal:

    ```bash
    dnz zone dnssec example.com on                # prints the DS record for the registrar
    ```

    DNZ creates the zone's key (one **ECDSA P-256, algorithm 13** key by default) and starts
    signing its answers right away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the DS record at your registrar">
    Copy the **DS record** shown and add it at your domain registrar (or in the parent zone). Most
    registrars ask for the *Key tag*, *Algorithm*, *Digest type* (2) and *Digest*. Some ask for
    the DNSKEY (public key) instead; the card shows both.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the chain of trust">
    Once the DS is published, run the [checks below](#check-it).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Until the DS is in place, the zone works as before, just unvalidated ("insecure").

<Warning>
  A signed zone answers every name in it itself, so [Forward missing
  names](/dns/zones#forward-missing-names) is turned off when you turn DNSSEC on, and can't be
  turned back on while the zone is signed.
</Warning>

## Signing settings

| Setting | Choices | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Algorithm** | ECDSA P-256 (13), Ed25519 (15), RSA/SHA-256 (8, 2048-bit keys) | ECDSA P-256 |
| **Keys** | One combined key (CSK, flags 257), or a KSK (flags 257) that signs the DNSKEY RRset and a ZSK (flags 256) that signs everything else | One combined key |
| **Proof of missing names** | Compact denial (RFC 9824) or NSEC3 (RFC 5155) | Compact denial |
| **NSEC3 iterations / salt** | 0–100, salt up to 32 bytes in hex | 0, no salt (RFC 9276) |
| **Roll the ZSK every** | Days, or 0 for only by hand (KSK + ZSK only) | 90 days |
| **Roll the KSK (or key) every** | Days, or 0 for only by hand | 0 |
| **Publish CDS/CDNSKEY** | On or off | On |

```bash
dnz zone dnssec example.com --algorithm ed25519 --keys ksk-zsk
dnz zone dnssec example.com --denial nsec3 --nsec3-iterations 0 --nsec3-salt -
dnz zone dnssec example.com --zsk-lifetime 60 --ksk-lifetime 365 --cds on
```

Ed25519 gives the smallest answers; RSA is there for parents and validators that need it. Changing
the algorithm or the key split of a signed zone starts a [rollover](#key-rollover); it can't be
changed again until that rollover has finished.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Compact denial or NSEC3">
    - **Compact denial** (RFC 9824, "black lies"): a missing name or type gets a NOERROR answer with
      an NSEC that covers only the queried name (type `NXNAME` for names that don't exist). Nothing
      is computed ahead and the zone's contents can't be walked. Some older validators and
      monitoring tools expect NXDOMAIN for missing names.
    - **NSEC3** (RFC 5155, opt-out off): missing names get a real NXDOMAIN with the closest encloser
      proof, missing types an NSEC3 matching the name, and wildcard answers are signed as the
      wildcard with the next closer name proven missing. The hashed chain is computed once per
      version of the zone, and the apex answers `NSEC3PARAM`.

    RFC 9276 asks for 0 extra NSEC3 iterations and no salt: neither makes hashed names much harder
    to guess, both cost resolvers work, and validators may treat high iteration counts as insecure.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Key rollover

DNZ rolls keys by itself; each step waits long enough for caches to forget what they had. The zone
page shows the rollover in progress, each key's state and when the next step is due. Start one by
hand with **Roll KSK / Roll ZSK / Roll CSK**, or:

```bash
dnz zone dnssec example.com --roll zsk        # csk with one key, ksk or zsk with two
```

- **ZSK** (pre-publish, RFC 6781): the new ZSK is published first, starts signing once the DNSKEY
  RRset's TTL (1 hour) plus a margin has passed, and the old one stays published until the zone's
  longest TTL plus a margin has passed. Nothing changes at the registrar.
- **KSK or combined key** (double signature): the new key signs next to the old one at once. Once
  caches have the new DNSKEY RRset, **CDS and CDNSKEY** (RFC 7344) switch to the new key and the zone
  page shows the new DS. The rollover then waits for the parent zone's DS to point at the new key.
- **Algorithm** (RFC 6781 section 4.1.4): the new keys sign everything before they are published,
  are published once the zone's longest TTL has passed, take over the DS like a KSK, and the old
  keys leave the DNSKEY RRset before they stop signing.
- **One key ↔ KSK + ZSK** with the same algorithm needs no new DS.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Update the DS">
    When the zone page says it's waiting for the parent, put the new DS at your registrar.
    Registrars and registries that read CDS (RFC 8078) do it by themselves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="DNZ notices">
    DNZ looks up the zone's DS through its upstreams every 15 minutes while it waits. Where that
    can't work (an internal zone), confirm with **The registrar has the new DS**, or:

    ```bash
    dnz zone dnssec example.com --ds-updated
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="The old key goes">
    Two days later (the parent's DS TTL plus a margin), the old key is removed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How it works

- **Online signing**: every answer is signed as it is sent, for clients that set the DO bit, by each
  key that signs that RRset. No zone needs re-signing and no signature expires unnoticed. Record
  changes are signed immediately.
- **DNSKEY, CDS and CDNSKEY** (and NSEC3PARAM with NSEC3) are answered automatically at the zone
  apex.
- **Views, wildcards, CNAMEs, DNAMEs and Auto PTR** are signed too; a CNAME chain that ends at a
  missing name carries the proof for that name.
- **Disabled records** and disabled zones are not signed (they aren't answered either).
- **Keys are kept** when DNSSEC is turned off, so turning it on again doesn't change the DS.
- **DNZ secondaries** of a signed zone receive the same keys from the primary, with their rollover
  states, so one DS covers every DNZ nameserver. Only the primary rolls keys. See
  [Replication](/dns/replication).

## Check it

```bash
dig +dnssec @192.0.2.10 example.com A          # RRSIG in the answer
dig @192.0.2.10 example.com DNSKEY
dig @192.0.2.10 example.com CDS                # the DS the parent should have
delv @1.1.1.1 example.com                      # once the DS is in place: "fully validated"
```

Before the DS is at the parent, validate against DNZ directly with the DS as a trust anchor (BIND
9.18's `delv`):

```bash
cat > anchor.conf <<'EOF'
trust-anchors { "example.com." static-ds 12345 13 2 "49FD46E6…"; };
EOF
delv @192.0.2.10 -a anchor.conf +root=example.com www.example.com A
```

Replace `192.0.2.10` with your DNZ server's address. [DNSViz](https://dnsviz.net) and the
[Verisign DNSSEC Debugger](https://dnssec-debugger.verisignlabs.com) work too.

## Turn it off

<Steps>
  <Step title="Remove the DS at the registrar">
    Delete the DS record at your registrar first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for its TTL">
    Wait for the DS record's TTL to run out, usually a day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn DNSSEC off in DNZ">
    Only then turn **DNSSEC** off on the zone page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  While the DS is still published but answers are no longer signed, validating resolvers reject the
  zone (SERVFAIL).
</Warning>

## Secondaries that aren't DNZ

Zones are transferred without signatures, because DNZ signs answers online. A BIND, Knot or
PowerDNS secondary therefore serves them unsigned. For signed zones, use DNZ secondaries (they share
the key), or sign on the secondary with its own key and add its DS too. See
[Replication](/dns/replication#dnz-as-primary-for-bind-knot-or-powerdns).
