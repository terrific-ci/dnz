---
title: "What changed?"
description: "One timeline of records, routes, rules, certificates, settings and nodes around an incident or in any window. It shows what happened before and never says what caused it."
---

**High availability → What changed?** puts what changed in DNZ on one timeline: records, routes, WAF
and firewall rules, certificates, settings, nodes joining or leaving. When something breaks, the
first question is usually what happened just before, and this answers it without opening five pages.

<Note>
It shows what happened before. **It never says what caused anything.** Lines about the incident's
route or name come first because they are the place to start looking, not because DNZ knows they
are to blame.
</Note>

Nothing here writes anything, and nothing runs while visitors or DNS clients are being served: DNZ
reads what it keeps anyway.

## Opening it

- **High availability → What changed?**, with a window, a name and a kind to filter on.
- **What changed?** on an open uptime incident, **What changed before it?** in the incident history,
  and on a proxy or forward DNS incident in the bell at the top of the page. The window then ends
  when the incident began, and the incident's names come first.
- From the terminal:

```bash
dnz changes                                   # the last 30 minutes
dnz changes --since 2h --near app.example.com # lines about the name first (marked *)
dnz changes --incident uptime:12              # the 30 minutes before that incident, its names first
dnz changes --since 7d --kind routes,firewall # records, routes, firewall, certificates, nodes, settings, incidents
```

- For AI assistants, the `list_changes` tool ([AI assistants](/integrations/ai-assistants)).

## What a line comes from

| Line | From | Who may see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Records and a zone's settings | The zone's [history](/dns/history) | *View zones* in the zone's division |
| Routes | The proxy's [route history](/proxy/route-history) | *Manage proxies* |
| WAF, firewalls and other middlewares | The audit log | *View audit log* in the entry's division; server-wide entries: *Manage proxies* |
| Certificates issued or renewed | Each certificate's progress log | *Manage proxies* |
| Nodes joining, leaving, moving to another version | The audit log | As the audit log's lines; server-wide entries: *Manage proxies* |
| Settings and everything else | The audit log | Superadmins for server-wide entries, *View audit log* for a division's |
| Earlier incidents | The incident list and uptime incidents | Who can read those incidents |

A line shows only if you may see its source, **division by division**: with *View zones* in one
division you get that division's zones and nothing of another's. An API token sees what its own
permissions read, and a token limited to some zones sees those zones only. The page says which
kinds your roles don't show, so a short list isn't mistaken for a quiet window.

## Near the cause first

Give a name, or open the timeline from an incident, and these come before the rest of the window,
each part newest first:

- route versions whose hostnames cover the name, a wildcard route included;
- zone versions that change a record of that name, or the settings of the zone it is in;
- middlewares, firewalls and upstreams of the routes serving it;
- certificates covering it, and anything else whose target mentions it.

## The window

The last 30 minutes by default, or the 30 minutes before the incident's start. Widen it to 2 hours,
24 hours or 7 days on the page, or anything up to 31 days with `--since`. Long lists come a page at
a time. If more changed than one answer reads (2,000 lines of each source), the page says so and
asks you to narrow the window.

## Diffs and restoring

Open a zone or route version to see its diff, as on the history pages. **History and rollback** and
**History and restore** lead to its history, where putting it back goes through the usual
confirmation and, for a zone, the usual change approval. An audit entry shows the before and after
it kept; a change of DNS filtering links to Filtering's [history](/dns/filtering#history), where one
change can be undone. Nothing is restored from the timeline by itself, and assistants can only read
it.

## API

`GET /api/what-changed` takes `since` (`30m`, `2h`, `7d` or a time), `until`, `near`, `incident`
(`uptime:<id>` or `alert:<id>`), `kind` (comma-separated), `q`, `division_id` (only narrows what you
may see), `page` and `per_page` (20 a page, at most 200). Each line has `id`, `source`, `kind`, `at`,
`actor`, `via`, `target`, `summary`, `near`, `action` and `links` (`page`, and for versions `diff`
and `restore` as API paths).
