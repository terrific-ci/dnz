The dashboard is a single-page app built into the `dnz` binary. It works offline (no fonts or
icons from a CDN), supports light and dark mode (charcoal or near-white, with blue for what you can
act on), and moves its menu to a bottom dock on phones.

## The sidebar

From top to bottom:

- **DNZ logo** and version: back to the Overview. The arrows beside it fold the sidebar to icons
  only (hover an icon for its name); DNZ remembers the choice in this browser.
- **Search menu** (press `/`): narrows the menu as you type. **Esc** clears it.
- **+**: Create New, a zone, user, role, division or notification provider, depending on what you
  may do.
- **Pin a zone**: star the zones you open most; they stay at the top of the sidebar for the division
  you're in. With nothing pinned it offers your first zones. Hidden without `zones.read` and in
  proxy-only mode.
- **The menu**, in groups that fold away (DNZ remembers which): DNS, Proxy, High availability,
  Settings. A group opens by itself when you go to one of its pages. Changes waiting for your
  approval and your number of zones show as counts.
- **This server**: its name, whether it is a primary or a secondary, how long it has been up, the
  address you reach it on with the round trip, and the primary it follows. Beside it sits Denz, DNZ's
  mascot: smiling while the forward DNS upstreams are up, worried while some are down.

## The top bar

From left to right:

- **Where you are**: the section and page.
- **Find** (`⌘K` on macOS, `Ctrl+K` elsewhere): jump to any page, zone or setting by typing. It
  also lists the settings cards inside the pages you may open (DNS cache, upstreams, blocklists,
  the block page, encrypted DNS, RRL, TSIG keys, MCP and more), each found by the words people use
  for it (`flush`, `adblock`, `2fa`…), and scrolls to the card. Every word you type must match, in
  any order. **Clear DNS cache** and **Your account: password and two-factor** run straight from
  Find.
- **Division switcher**: the division you're working in. Zones, members, tokens and logs follow it.
  You only see the divisions you're assigned to; superadmins see them all.
- **Health** (superadmins): whether the forward DNS upstreams are up, which one is resolving now
  and which takes over next.
- **Incidents** (superadmins): upstream outages and recoveries.
- **Account menu**: account settings (profile, password, signed-in sessions), the theme (System,
  Light or Dark), sign out, and **About DNZ** with the version.

Older links to Topology, Access, DNS → Replication and Settings → Cluster still open the page at its
new place.

## Denz

Denz, the round blue name server on the sign-in page, setup, the page for an address that doesn't
exist and a few empty lists, is there for fun and changes nothing. It follows your pointer, reads
along while you type your username, covers its eyes while you type your password (and peeks when
you pause), warns about Caps Lock, shakes its head at a wrong password and cheers when you're in.
Click it for a reaction; click it fast and it gets dizzy. Left alone for half a minute it dozes off.
With **reduce motion** turned on in your system it stays still and only blinks.

## Right-click menus

Lists with actions open a menu on right-click (or a long press on touch screens): zones, records,
proxies, users, filtering rules, replication, tokens and the rest. It has the same actions as the
row's **⋯** menu, plus **Open in new tab** for rows that open a page and **Copy** for names,
values and addresses. A record's menu also has **Duplicate**, which opens **Add record** filled in
from it. On the topology map, right-click a node to open or copy it, or the empty canvas to fit the
view, lay it out again or export it. Where a row has no actions for you (for example on a
secondary), the browser's own menu shows instead.

## Overview

The landing page sums up the current division:

- **Traffic**: queries over the chosen range (24 hours to 90 days), with answered, NXDOMAIN and
  SERVFAIL counts. A division's traffic is the names in its zones plus everything clients in its
  [client networks](/dns/filtering) ask. Superadmins get a **Scope** switch: **All queries** (their
  default) counts the whole server, including names forwarded for clients in no client network,
  such as google.com from a laptop no division claims. The other choice shows just the division.
  Answered by, the top lists, Recent queries and the links into the query log follow the scope.
- **Answered by**: how many of those queries were answered from your zones (**Local**), sent to an
  upstream or a forwarding rule (**Forwarded**), served from the **Cache**, **Blocked**, or ended in
  an **Error**, each with its share. Click one to open the query log filtered to it.
- **Top names and clients**, each linking into the query log.
- **Request flow**: how queries travel from clients through filtering, the cache and forwarding
  rules to the upstreams. With proxying on, it has two tabs: **DNS** (that path) and **Through a
  proxy**, which shows visitors → each proxy → its routers and middlewares → upstreams → servers.
  The button at its top right shows it full screen (Esc leaves).
- **Zones**: a card per zone.
- **Recent queries**.
- **Alerts**: recent upstream outages.

Once proxying is on, a **Proxy** tab beside **DNS** shows the proxies at a glance, for those who may
manage proxies:

- **Traffic** over the last 24 hours through the DNZ Proxy nodes: visitors active now, requests,
  bandwidth, cache hit ratio, 5xx rate, blocked requests and p95 latency, requests by status class,
  and the top hostnames and status codes. Once a DNZ Proxy counts [peak concurrency](/proxy/dnz-proxy#peak-concurrency)
  (off by default), a **Peak concurrent** tile shows the most requests in progress and connections
  open at once. The full view is [Proxy → Analytics](/proxy/dnz-proxy#analytics).
- **Proxies**: each proxy with its nodes up, and nodes waiting for approval.
- **Health**: upstream servers a node finds down ([Server health](/proxy/upstreams#server-health)),
  certificates failing or expiring within 14 days, and the latest proxy alerts.

Traefik and Caddy don't report their traffic, so only their health shows.

A **Topology** tab draws the whole setup on one canvas; see [Topology map](/dashboard/topology).
Superadmins also see **forward DNS monitoring** ([Monitoring](/dashboard/monitoring)). A **Server** tab
shows this server, its secondaries and every proxy with its nodes, each for those who may see it; see
[Server](/dashboard/server). Once the server has secondaries or copies zones from a
primary, a **Replication** tab shows each replica's state and how far it is in sync, part by part
(zones, record details, settings, accounts); see [Sync status](/dns/replication#sync-status).
