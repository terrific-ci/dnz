---

DNZ is **one binary** that answers DNS for your own zones, forwards everything else to upstream
resolvers, filters ads and malware, replicates zones to other nameservers and can put your sites
behind a reverse proxy. You manage it from a dashboard with a Cloudflare-like record editor, from
the `dnz` command line, or over its HTTP API.

It is built for IT teams that run DNS for internal networks: a hospital, a campus, an office with
several departments. Each department is a **division** with its own zones, members and permissions.

## What DNZ does

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Authoritative DNS" icon="globe">
    UDP and TCP with EDNS(0). Zones live in the database and are kept in memory: A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, TXT,
    NS, SRV, CAA, HTTPS/SVCB, PTR, TLSA, SSHFP, NAPTR, DS, URI, DNAME, LOC and wildcards, with an automatic SOA, CNAME chasing and negative
    answers. A local zone can also override just a few names of a public domain and forward the
    rest. See [Zones and records](/dns/zones).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Forwarding with health checks" icon="route">
    Names outside your zones go to upstreams over plain DNS, DoT, DoH, DoQ or DoH3, with a cache,
    five load-balancing strategies, conditional forwarding per domain or client network, latency
    monitoring and a step-by-step diagnosis when an upstream fails. See
    [Forwarding](/dns/forwarding).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Filtering" icon="shield-halved">
    Server-wide and per-division blocklists (hosts, AdGuard and domain-list formats), your own allow
    and block rules, and a tester that tells you why a domain is blocked. Safe search, parental
    control, schedules, rules for some clients only, and response policy zones (RPZ). See
    [Filtering](/dns/filtering) and [Response policy zones](/dns/rpz).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="GeoDNS and health checks" icon="earth-asia">
    Answer plain A and AAAA records by the client's country or continent, by weight, and only while
    an HTTP, HTTPS or TCP health check passes. See [GeoDNS and health checks](/dns/geodns).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Split horizon, DNSSEC and encrypted DNS" icon="lock">
    Answer one name differently inside and outside, sign zones with DNSSEC, and serve DoT, DoH, DoQ
    and DoH3 to your devices, with response rate limiting (RRL) against amplification. See
    [Views](/dns/views), [DNSSEC](/dns/dnssec), [Encrypted DNS](/dns/encrypted-dns) and
    [Rate limiting](/dns/rate-limiting).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Replication" icon="clone">
    DNZ as primary and/or secondary with AXFR/IXFR, TSIG, NOTIFY and a catalog zone. DNZ servers
    find each other on the LAN and join with a 6-digit code. See [Replication](/dns/replication).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Proxied records" icon="cloud">
    DNS answers with a proxy's address and the origin stays hidden.
    Use Traefik, Caddy, or DNZ's own Pingora-based **dnz-proxy** with certificates, caching, a
    firewall and analytics, and mix them. See [Proxy](/proxy/overview).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Automation and integrations" icon="plug">
    API tokens, ACME DNS-01 for lego, Traefik and certbot, DynDNS2 for routers, RFC 2136 dynamic
    updates for Kea and ExternalDNS, Cloudflare sync, and notifications to Telegram, e-mail and
    webhooks.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Access control and audit" icon="users">
    Divisions, roles with fine-grained permissions, superadmins, and an audit log of every change.
    See [Users, divisions and roles](/admin/access).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## One program

DNZ is a single program with the dashboard built in. It keeps all of its data in one
SQLite file, so a backup is one file, or in [Postgres, MySQL or Redis](/deploy/database) for large
installations. It runs on Linux (amd64 and arm64), as a
[Docker image](/deploy/docker) or a [systemd service](/deploy/systemd). The optional reverse
proxy, `dnz-proxy`, comes with it.

## Source and releases

DNZ is on GitHub at [terrific-ci/dnz](https://github.com/terrific-ci/dnz). Every release is
published there with its binaries for Linux amd64 and arm64, next to the install script, the
Prometheus and Grafana files, the benchmark harnesses and the dnz-proxy Swarm stack. Report bugs
and ask for features in its [issues](https://github.com/terrific-ci/dnz/issues).
