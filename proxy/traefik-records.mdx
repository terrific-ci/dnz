A Traefik often gets routers from its other providers too. DNZ can read them and give their
hostnames DNS records, so a new service gets its name in DNS by itself. For example:

- a new Swarm service with ``traefik.http.routers.app.rule=Host(`app.example.com`)``, or
- a new Kubernetes Ingress for `app.example.com`

DNZ reads routers from **every** provider: **Docker Swarm** service labels, **Docker** container
labels, **Kubernetes** (Ingress, IngressRoute / IngressRouteTCP, Gateway API), the **file**
provider, **Consul Catalog**, **Nomad**, **ECS**, another **HTTP** provider, and so on.

It works whether proxying is on or not. The records it makes are DNS only: they answer with the
Traefik's addresses directly.

## Turning it on

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the proxy's Traefik API">
    The feature reads routers through Traefik's API. In the proxy dialog, fill in **Traefik API**.
    See [Traefik's API](/proxy/traefik#traefiks-api).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn on the switch">
    Open the proxy, go to **From Traefik** and turn on **Records from Traefik's routers**. It is off
    by default.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the list">
    DNZ reads the Traefik's HTTP and TCP routers right away, then every 30 seconds. **Sync now** reads
    them again at once. The tab shows which providers the names came from (`swarm`, `kubernetescrd`,
    `file`, …), and each router keeps its `@provider` suffix.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Which routers count

- DNZ's own routers (`dnz-…`) and Traefik's internal ones (`@internal`) are left out, and so are
  routers Traefik turned off.
- From each rule DNZ takes the names of `Host`, `HostHeader` and `HostSNI`. A rule can give several,
  joined with `||` or listed in one matcher.
- `HostRegexp` gives a wildcard when it is one label of anything in front of a fixed domain:
  `^[a-z0-9-]+\.apps\.example\.com$` becomes `*.apps.example.com`.
- Negated matchers (`!Host(…)`) are ignored.
- Rules without a usable name, such as ``PathPrefix(`/`)``, ``HostSNI(`*`)`` or other patterns, are
  listed under **routers left out**, with the reason.

### Names without a domain

Providers that make a default rule from the service name (Docker, Swarm, Consul Catalog, Nomad,
ECS: ``Host(`{{ normalize .Name }}`)``) give a name without a domain, which DNS can't answer for.
Such routers are listed under **routers left out**.

Give the provider a `defaultRule` with your domain, or set a rule per service:

```yaml traefik.yml
providers:
  docker:
    defaultRule: "Host(`{{ normalize .Name }}.example.com`)"
```

## What DNZ makes

- Each name goes in the local primary zone it falls in, the longest one when zones are nested. A name
  with no zone here is listed as **No zone**.
- The name gets **DNS-only** A and AAAA records with the proxy's addresses, the zone's default TTL,
  and a comment saying where they came from.
- When the proxy's addresses change, the records follow.

Every change is in the [audit log](/admin/audit-log) as `record.create` or `record.update` by
*Traefik (proxy name)*, with the routers it came from.

## What DNZ leaves alone

| Status | When | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Has own records** | The name already has A, AAAA or CNAME records: yours, proxied ones, or another proxy's. DNZ never touches them. | Nothing needed |
| **Orphaned** | No router routes the name any more. Its records stay. | **⋯ → Delete its records**. If the router comes back first, nothing changes. |
| **Not managed** | You deleted its records by hand. They are not made again. | **⋯ → Manage again** |
| Released | You chose **⋯ → Keep as manual records**: DNZ let go of the name and its records are ordinary ones now. | **⋯ → Manage again** |

Turning the switch off, or a Traefik that can't be reached, changes no records. The error is shown on
the tab.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A router is listed under routers left out">
    Its rule gives no name DNS can answer for: a path-only rule, ``HostSNI(`*`)``, a pattern that
    isn't one label in front of a fixed domain, or a default rule without your domain. The reason is
    shown next to it. Fix the rule in Traefik, or give the provider a `defaultRule` with your domain.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A name shows No zone">
    No local primary zone in DNZ holds the name. Add the zone, or route a name inside one of DNZ's
    zones.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Nothing changes and the tab shows an error">
    DNZ can't read the Traefik's API. Check the **Traefik API** address and credentials in the proxy
    dialog, and that DNZ can reach that port.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

The API for this feature is under `/api/proxies/{id}/auto-records`. See
[Proxy API](/api/proxy#records-from-traefiks-routers).
